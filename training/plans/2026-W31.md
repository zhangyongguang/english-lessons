# Weekly Speaking Training · 2026-W31

## Focus

This week trains three recurring foundations from the evidence window 2026-W29 through 2026-W31. W31 contains the July 28-30 and August 1-2 lesson data.

## 1. Verb form after another verb or preposition

**Trigger:** building a verb after a modal, another verb, or a preposition.

**Why now:** W31 added 30 verb-form errors across five lesson days; the three-week window contains 81 across 18 lesson days.

**Real patterns:**

- “They can came back to the society.” → “They can come back to society.”
- “You can't expect them pay the same taxes.” → “You can't expect them to pay the same taxes.”
- “It can help our society more stable.” → “It can help our society become more stable.”

**Training cue:** after a modal use the base verb; after expect/ask/help use the correct complement; after `to` use the base verb.

**Pass condition:** at least 8 correct first attempts out of 10, one correct delayed retest, and correct use in two transfer contexts.

## 2. Precise word form and collocation

**Trigger:** choosing among related forms or near-synonyms when naming a thing, action, quality, or relationship.

**Why now:** W31 added 18 word-choice errors across four lesson days; the three-week window contains 67 across 16 lesson days.

**Real patterns:**

- “The economic will be pretty good.” → “The economy will be pretty good.”
- “Which situation can arrive more?” → “Which situation can thrive more?”
- “He could borrow a lot of money to a teacher.” → “He could lend a lot of money to a teacher.”

**Training cue:** identify the meaning and grammatical job first, then choose the exact word.

**Pass condition:** at least 8 correct first attempts out of 10, one correct delayed retest, and correct use in two transfer contexts.

## 3. Countable nouns and quantifiers

**Trigger:** talking about more than one countable person or thing, or choosing `much/many`, `some`, `enough`, `most of`, or `a lot of`.

**Why now:** W31 added 23 related plural, quantifier, and determiner errors across four lesson days; the three-week window contains 72 across 13 lesson days.

**Real patterns:**

- “They had too many hope.” → “They had too much hope.”
- “If they don't have enough consumer...” → “If they don't have enough consumers...”
- “Most of parents expected their children...” → “Most of the parents expected their children...”

**Training cue:** decide whether the noun is countable and singular/plural before choosing the quantifier.

**Pass condition:** at least 8 correct first attempts out of 10, one correct delayed retest, and correct use in two transfer contexts.

## Five-session progression

| Session | Main purpose | Prompt support |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Free production, notice, self-correction, complete restatement | Moderate |
| Day 2 | Retrieve the forms in unseen work and family questions | Minimal |
| Day 3 | Transfer to money, travel, and community topics | Minimal |
| Day 4 | Mix all three targets under follow-up pressure | None unless meaning fails |
| Day 5 | Unprompted assessment with unseen questions | None |

## Instructions for Chat Live

Use `training/live/current.md` as the complete 15–20 minute lesson prompt. Track only these three targets, count first attempts separately from prompted repairs, include delayed retrieval, and finish with the required Training report.

## Weekly decision rule

A target may enter spaced review only after at least 80% first-attempt accuracy, one delayed retest, and use in two contexts. Immediate repetition after hearing the answer does not count as mastery.
